How to plan an elopement wedding

An elopement wedding is a small, intimate marriage celebration focused solely on the couple rather than a large traditional guest list. Eloping typically involves just the two of you, an officiant, and a photographer, offering a simpler and often more affordable alternative to a big wedding.

Published 3 November 2026 · Dream Wedding Builder

Choose your dream elopement location

The first step in planning your elopement is deciding where you want to say your vows. Unlike traditional weddings tied to a local venue, an elopement can happen almost anywhere in the world. You might choose a scenic mountain overlook, a quiet beach at sunrise, a lush forest, or even the steps of a historic city courthouse. Think about what kind of backdrop feels most meaningful to your relationship. If you love hiking, pick a trail with a breathtaking view. If you prefer a stress-free urban vibe, look at city halls with stunning architecture.

Once you narrow down your general location, research the specific logistics. Some public parks and national forests require special permits for wedding ceremonies and photography, even for small groups. Check local rules months in advance so you avoid any last-minute surprises. If you are traveling out of state or internationally, confirm the travel requirements and accessibility of your chosen spot. Make sure you have a backup plan in case bad weather ruins your outdoor ceremony site.

Understand the marriage license and legal requirements

Every elopement requires legal paperwork to make your marriage official. The rules depend entirely on where you plan to exchange your vows. Start by checking the county clerk website or government office for the area where your ceremony will take place. Find out how long before the wedding date you must apply for the license and whether there is a mandatory waiting period. Some regions require a brief residency period, while others let you apply online and pick up the document a few days before the ceremony.

You will also need to hire an officiant who is legally authorized to perform marriages in that specific jurisdiction. Many elopement photographers are ordained and can legally sign your marriage license, which saves you from hiring an extra person. Do not forget that you need at least one or two witnesses in most states or countries to sign the paperwork. If you are eloping completely alone, ask your photographer and videographer to act as your official witnesses.

Build a simple elopement timeline and budget

Even though an elopement is much simpler than a traditional wedding, you still need a clear timeline to keep your day running smoothly. Plan your ceremony around the best lighting for photos, which is usually right after sunrise or during the hour before sunset. Give yourselves plenty of time to get ready without rushing, travel to the location, exchange your vows, and take portraits. If you are hiking to a remote spot, factor in extra time for the walk and changing clothes once you arrive.

Your budget for an elopement will be much smaller, but costs still add up. Focus your spending on the elements that matter most to you, such as a professional photographer, a beautiful bouquet, travel expenses, and a special dinner afterward. You can skip traditional expenses like large catering bills, dj services, and favors. Decide who to invite and how to celebrate

One of the biggest perks of an elopement is the freedom to invite zero guests. However, many couples choose to include a tiny circle of immediate family members or best friends. Keep your guest count under ten people if you want to keep the day intimate and easy to manage logistically. If you choose to keep your elopement a private event with just the two of you, plan a fun way to share the news with your loved ones afterward.

After the ceremony, celebrate in a way that fits your personal style. You might book a table at a fancy local restaurant, order takeout to eat on your hotel balcony, or spend the evening stargazing by a campfire. Bring a small keepsake or write personal letters to read to each other during the day. Remember that your elopement day is entirely about your bond, so design an experience that feels authentic and joyful to you.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need witnesses for an elopement?

Most states and countries require one or two witnesses to sign the marriage license alongside the couple and the officiant. Check the local laws where you plan to elope to see how many witnesses are legally required for your ceremony.

Can we wear traditional wedding attire for an elopement?

You can wear whatever you want for your elopement. Many couples wear traditional wedding dresses and suits, while others choose casual dresses, jumpsuits, or hiking gear depending on their ceremony location and personal style.

How far in advance should we plan an elopement?

You can plan an elopement in just a few weeks, but three to six months gives you enough time to secure travel arrangements, book a great photographer, apply for permits, and handle marriage license paperwork without stress.

How do we tell family we eloped?

Share the news with close family members shortly after the ceremony through a phone call or a nice announcement card. You can also host a casual party or dinner later on to celebrate with friends and family who could not attend.
