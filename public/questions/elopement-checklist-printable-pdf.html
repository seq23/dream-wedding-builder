What are the first steps on your elopement timeline?

Start your elopement planning six to nine months out by picking a general region or destination. Talk about what kind of scenery you want. Do you picture a snowy mountain peak, a windy coastal cliff, or a quiet city hall? Once you agree on the setting, look up the marriage laws for that specific county or country. Some places have mandatory waiting periods or residency requirements that affect your dates.

Next, set a realistic budget for your day. Even though elopements cost less than traditional weddings, you still spend money on travel, photography, attire, and permits. Book your photographer early if you want someone specific to capture your vows. Photographers often double as local guides who know the best hidden spots. Four to six months before the big day, secure your special use permits if you are marrying on public land. National parks and state forests usually require paperwork and fees for ceremonies, even tiny ones. Order your wedding attire with plenty of time for alterations. Outdoor elopements often require sturdy footwear, so break in your hiking boots or shoes well before the ceremony date to prevent blisters.

How to handle legal requirements and witnesses

The legal side of eloping is the most important part of the day. Without the right paperwork, the ceremony is just a lovely commitment. Research the county clerk office where you will legally marry. Find out if you need an appointment to pick up your marriage license. Note the expiration date of the license, as many states require you to use it within thirty to sixty days of issuance.

Check the witness laws for your elopement location. Some states require two witnesses to sign your license, while others require only one or even none. If you are eloping alone with just the two of you, you can ask your photographer and your officiant to sign as witnesses. If you prefer a completely private elopement with zero guests, you must plan ahead to find willing witnesses near your ceremony site.

An officiant is another legal requirement in most jurisdictions. You might hire a local celebrant, an elopement photographer who is also ordained, or a friend who gets ordained online. Confirm local rules to ensure online ordination is accepted in your chosen county. Keep all your legal documents in a waterproof folder so they stay crisp and safe while you travel to your ceremony spot.

What should you pack in your elopement day bag?

On your elopement day, you will likely be far away from a home base or hotel. Packing the right gear ensures you stay comfortable through wind, rain, or bright sun. Bring a sturdy backpack to carry your essentials. Pack your marriage license, valid photo IDs, and rings in a secure, waterproof pouch. Do not leave these critical items in a car trunk where temperatures fluctuate.

Bring practical comfort items that match your environment. Pack plenty of water, high-energy snacks, tissues for happy tears, bug spray, and sunscreen. If you are hiking to your ceremony site, bring comfortable walking shoes and carry your nicer wedding shoes in a protective bag until you arrive at the spot. Pack a basic emergency kit with stain remover wipes, bobby pins, safety pins, and pain relievers.

Weather can change quickly in outdoor settings. Pack layers like a stylish jacket, thermal base layers under your clothes if it is cold, and clear umbrellas if rain is in the forecast. Frequently asked questions

Do I need an officiant to elope?

Yes, most locations require an authorized officiant to legally marry you during your elopement ceremony. This person can be a local professional, an ordained friend, or sometimes a registered elopement photographer. Always check the specific laws of the county or country where you plan to exchange your vows.

How many witnesses do I need for an elopement?

The number of witnesses required depends entirely on your specific elopement location. Some jurisdictions require two adult witnesses to sign the marriage license, while others require only one or none at all. You can easily ask your photographer or your local guide to serve as your official witnesses.

Can I elope anywhere in a national park?

No, national parks restrict elopements and small ceremonies to designated areas to protect natural resources. You must almost always apply for a special use permit months in advance and pay a fee. Violating these rules can result in fines or getting shut down on your wedding day.

What should I pack in my elopement day bag?

You should pack your physical marriage license, IDs, wedding rings, water, snacks, bug spray, sunscreen, and weather layers. Include practical items like a first aid kit, stain remover wipes, and a change of comfortable shoes if you are hiking to your ceremony location.
