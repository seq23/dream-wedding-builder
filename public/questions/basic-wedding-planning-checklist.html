Basic Wedding Planning Checklist

A basic wedding planning checklist is a step-by-step master guide that breaks the entire wedding journey down into manageable tasks ordered by month, helping you book venues, hire vendors, manage your budget, and track RSVPs without feeling overwhelmed by the process.

Published 3 November 2026 · Dream Wedding Builder

How do I start using a basic wedding planning checklist?

Start by sitting down with your partner to talk about two major items: your total budget and your estimated guest count. These two numbers drive every single decision you make on your journey. Once you agree on what you can spend and roughly how many people will attend, open up your calendar and set a target wedding date. You can then download our Free Wedding Planning Checklist PDF — Wedding Checklist PDF to map out your months.

Next, break your overall budget into realistic categories. Allocate roughly half of your funds to your venue and catering, since food and space take up the largest share of wedding costs. Set aside smaller percentages for photography, attire, entertainment, flowers, and stationery. Keep a five percent buffer in your budget for surprise expenses like unexpected alterations, service fees, or extra postage. Write these numbers down in a dedicated spreadsheet or notebook so you can track actual spending against your estimates as you go.

What tasks belong on the twelve-month timeline?

The year before your wedding requires big-picture decisions that book up fast. Secure your ceremony and reception venues first, as your date is not official until you sign a contract and pay a deposit. Draft your guest list early so you know your venue capacity will actually fit your crowd. Research and book high-priority vendors who can only work one wedding per day, such as your photographer, videographer, band or DJ, and caterer if your venue does not provide food.

During these early months, you should also choose your wedding party and start looking at wedding dress styles. Gowns often take six to nine months to arrive after ordering, so shopping early prevents rush shipping fees and stress. What should I focus on six to nine months before the wedding?

As the six-month mark approaches, turn your attention to the details that bring your day together. Send out save-the-dates if you are hosting a destination wedding or have many out-of-town guests traveling during peak holiday seasons. Otherwise, standard save-the-dates go out four to six months in advance. Shop for bridesmaid dresses and groomsmen attire, making sure everyone knows their ordering deadlines and color palettes.

Book your florist, cake baker, and any rental companies you need for chairs, tables, or linens. Begin planning your honeymoon if you plan to travel right after the wedding, including booking flights and hotels while availability is open. How do I handle the final three months before the wedding?

The final quarter is all about final counts, schedules, and tying up loose ends. Mail your formal wedding invitations roughly eight weeks before the wedding, setting your RSVP deadline for three to four weeks before the big day. This gives you time to finalize your seating chart and give exact guest headcounts to your caterer and venue coordinator.

Schedule your dress fittings and tuxedo rentals to ensure everything fits comfortably. Buy your wedding bands, write your vows, and apply for your marriage license according to your local county clerk rules. Create a minute-by-minute timeline for the wedding day itself, and share it with your vendors, bridal party, and family members so everyone knows where to be and when.

Frequently asked questions

What should be on a basic wedding planning checklist?

A basic wedding planning checklist should include your budget breakdown, venue bookings, guest list creation, vendor selection timelines, invitation mailing dates, and day-of schedules. Breaking these tasks into monthly steps ensures you never miss crucial deadlines while planning your celebration.

When should I send out wedding invitations?

You should mail your formal wedding invitations six to eight weeks before your wedding date. Set your RSVP deadline for three to four weeks before the wedding so you have enough time to finalize your final headcounts for your caterer and venue.

How do I create a wedding budget without a planner?

To create a wedding budget without a planner, list your total available funds from all contributors. Research average local costs for venues, food, and photography, then assign realistic dollar amounts to each category while keeping a small buffer for unexpected expenses.

When should I book my wedding venue?

You should book your wedding venue twelve to eighteen months before your wedding date, especially if you want to marry during peak weekend seasons. Securing the venue first locks in your official date so you can safely book all other vendors.
